1. Surface Tension ($S$ or $T$)

Molecular theory, free surfaces, surface energy, excess pressure & capillary ascent

Definition & Units

Surface tension is the property by virtue of which a liquid surface behaves like a stretched elastic membrane trying to minimize its surface area.

$$S = \frac{F}{l}$$
  • • SI Unit: $\text{N/m}$ or $\text{J/m}^2$
  • • Dimensions: $[M^1 L^0 T^{-2}]$
  • • CGS Unit: $\text{dyne/cm}$ ($1\text{ N/m} = 10^3\text{ dyne/cm}$)

Factors Affecting Surface Tension

Temperature: As $T \uparrow$, $S \downarrow$. At critical temperature $T_c$, $S = 0$.
Highly Soluble Impurities (e.g. NaCl): Surface tension increases ($S \uparrow$).
Sparsely Soluble / Detergents: Surface tension decreases ($S \downarrow$).

Free Surfaces & Area Factor (Crucial for Numericals)

Structure / System Free Surfaces ($N$) Total Area Factor Excess Pressure ($\Delta P$)
Liquid Drop 1 $A = 4\pi R^2$ $\Delta P = \frac{2S}{R}$
Soap Bubble in Air 2 $A = 2 \times (4\pi R^2) = 8\pi R^2$ $\Delta P = \frac{4S}{R}$
Air Bubble Inside Liquid 1 $A = 4\pi R^2$ $\Delta P = \frac{2S}{R}$
Cylindrical Liquid Column 1 $A = 2\pi R L$ $\Delta P = \frac{S}{R}$

Work Done & Surface Energy

Work done to increase surface area by $\Delta A_{\text{total}}$ is stored as surface potential energy:

$$W = \Delta U = S \cdot \Delta A_{\text{total}}$$

Blowing Soap Bubble of Radius $R$:

$W = S \times (2 \times 4\pi R^2) = \mathbf{8\pi R^2 S}$

Work to Double Radius ($R \to 2R$):

$W_{\text{add}} = 8\pi S \left[(2R)^2 - R^2\right] = \mathbf{24\pi R^2 S} = 3 W_{\text{initial}}$

Droplet Coalescence vs Splitting

When $n$ tiny droplets of radius $r$ merge into a big drop of radius $R$:

  • • Volume Conservation: $\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3 = n \cdot \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \implies \mathbf{R = n^{1/3} r}$
  • • Area Reduction: Surface area decreases $\implies$ Energy released (Exothermic process).
  • • Temperature Rise ($\Delta T$):
$$\Delta U = 4\pi S (n r^2 - R^2) = 3 S V \left(\frac{1}{r} - \frac{1}{R}\right)$$ $$\Delta T = \frac{3S}{J \rho s} \left(\frac{1}{r} - \frac{1}{R}\right)$$

Contact Angle ($\theta$) & Capillary Rise (Ascent Formula)

Acute ($\theta < 90^\circ$)

Concave meniscus, liquid wets glass, liquid rises in capillary (e.g. Water in Glass, $\theta \approx 0^\circ$).

Right Angle ($\theta = 90^\circ$)

Flat meniscus, liquid neither rises nor falls (e.g. Water in Silver tube).

Obtuse ($\theta > 90^\circ$)

Convex meniscus, non-wetting liquid, liquid level falls in capillary (e.g. Mercury in Glass, $\theta \approx 140^\circ$).

Ascent Formula (Height of Rise $h$)

$$h = \frac{2 S \cos\theta}{r \rho g} = \frac{2S}{R \rho g}$$

Where $r$ = tube radius, $R = \frac{r}{\cos\theta}$ = meniscus curvature radius.

Jurin's Law & Tube of Insufficient Length

Product of height and radius of meniscus is constant: $\mathbf{h \cdot R = \text{Constant}}$.
If a capillary of length $h' < h$ is taken, liquid WILL NOT OVERFLOW. Instead, meniscus flattens ($R' > R$) such that $\mathbf{h' R' = h R}$.

Energy Dissipation Derivation in Capillary Rise:

• Work done by Surface Tension: $W_{\text{ST}} = (2\pi r S \cos\theta) \cdot h = \mathbf{\frac{4\pi S^2 \cos^2\theta}{\rho g}}$
• Gain in Potential Energy of liquid column: $\Delta U = m g \left(\frac{h}{2}\right) = (\pi r^2 h \rho) g \frac{h}{2} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{2} W_{\text{ST}}}$
• Heat Loss ($\Delta H$): $W_{\text{ST}} - \Delta U = \mathbf{\frac{1}{2} W_{\text{ST}}}$. Exactly 50% of the work done is converted into heat!

2. Elasticity & Hooke's Law

Stress, Strain, Elastic Moduli ($Y, \eta, B$), Wire Elongation, Fast Loading & Poisson's Ratio

Longitudinal

Young's Modulus ($Y$)

Applies only to solids.

$$Y = \frac{\text{Longitudinal Stress}}{\text{Longitudinal Strain}} = \frac{F / A}{\Delta L / L}$$
Tangential

Shear Modulus ($\eta$)

Modulus of Rigidity.

$$\eta = \frac{\text{Shear Stress}}{\text{Shear Strain}} = \frac{F_\parallel / A}{\theta}$$
Volumetric

Bulk Modulus ($B$)

Solids, Liquids & Gases.

$$B = - V \frac{\Delta P}{\Delta V} \quad \left[\text{Compressibility } k = \frac{1}{B}\right]$$

Wire Elongation & Own Weight

Elongation due to External Load $F$:
$$\Delta L = \frac{F L}{A Y}$$
Elongation due to Own Weight ($M, L, \rho$):

Center of mass moves by $L/2$. Average force = $Mg/2$.

$$\Delta L_{\text{weight}} = \frac{M g L}{2 A Y} = \frac{\rho g L^2}{2 Y}$$

Slow (Gradual) vs Sudden Loading

Slow / Gradual Loading:

Average force = $F/2$. Max extension $\Delta L = \frac{FL}{AY}$. Work done = $\frac{1}{2} F \Delta L = \mathbf{\Delta U}$ (No heat loss).

Sudden Loading (Weight dropped without velocity):

• Maximum Extension: $\mathbf{\Delta L_{\text{max}} = \frac{2 F L}{A Y}} = 2 \times \Delta L_{\text{slow}}$
• Maximum Stress: $\mathbf{\sigma_{\text{max}} = 2 \times \sigma_{\text{slow}}}$
• Work Done = $F \cdot \Delta L_{\text{max}} = \mathbf{\frac{2 F^2 L}{A Y}}$
• Elastic energy at maximum extension: $\Delta U = \mathbf{\frac{2F^2 L}{A Y}}$. In an ideal undamped model, there is no heat loss and the load can oscillate.

Poisson's Ratio ($\sigma$) & Elastic Constant Interrelations

Poisson's Ratio Definition:

$$\sigma = -\frac{\text{Lateral Strain}}{\text{Longitudinal Strain}} = -\frac{\Delta r / r}{\Delta L / L}$$
  • • Theoretical Limits: $-1 < \sigma < 0.5$
  • • Practical Limits: $0 < \sigma < 0.5$ (For most metals $\approx 0.3$)
  • • Volumetric Strain Relation: $\mathbf{\frac{\Delta V}{V} = \frac{\Delta L}{L}(1 - 2\sigma)}$
  • • If $\sigma = 0.5$ (Ideal incompressible elastic solid): $\frac{\Delta V}{V} = 0$. Volume does not change under stress!

The 4 Golden Moduli Interrelations (Must Remember):

1. $Y = 3B(1 - 2\sigma)$
2. $Y = 2\eta(1 + \sigma)$
3. $\sigma = \frac{3B - 2\eta}{2\eta + 6B}$
4. $\frac{9}{Y} = \frac{1}{B} + \frac{3}{\eta}$

Torsional Rigidity Constant ($C$):

Restoring torque $\tau = C \cdot \theta$.

• Solid Cylinder: $\mathbf{C = \frac{\pi \eta R^4}{2 L}}$

• Hollow Cylinder: $\mathbf{C = \frac{\pi \eta (R_2^4 - R_1^4)}{2 L}}$

Sagging of a Beam ($\delta$):

Depression $\delta$ at the center of a beam of length $L$, breadth $b$, depth $d$ loaded with weight $W$:

$$\delta = \frac{W L^3}{4 b d^3 Y}$$

3. Viscosity & Fluid Dynamics

Newton's law, Poiseuille conversions, Stokes' law, Terminal velocity derivation & Reynolds number

Newton's Law of Viscosity

Viscous force opposing relative layer motion is proportional to area $A$ and velocity gradient $\frac{dv}{dy}$:

$$F = -\eta A \frac{dv}{dy}$$

Unit Conversions (High JEE Error Rate!):

• SI Unit: $\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$ or $\text{N}\cdot\text{s/m}^2$ or $\text{kg/(m}\cdot\text{s)}$ (Poiseuille, $\text{Pl}$)

• CGS Unit: $\text{Poise} = \text{dyne}\cdot\text{s/cm}^2$

$$\mathbf{1 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s} = 1 \text{ Poiseuille} = 10 \text{ Poise}} \implies 1 \text{ Poise} = 0.1 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$$

Effect of Temperature on Viscosity

Liquids: $\eta \downarrow$ as $T \uparrow$

Dominant mechanism = Cohesive forces. Higher kinetic energy breaks intermolecular bonds $\implies$ Viscosity decreases.

Gases: $\eta \uparrow$ as $T \uparrow$ ($\eta \propto \sqrt{T}$)

Dominant mechanism = Momentum exchange between layers due to thermal motion. Higher speed $\implies$ more collisions $\implies$ Viscosity increases.

Stokes' Law & Terminal Velocity Derivation

When a spherical body of radius $r$ and density $\rho_s$ falls through a viscous medium of density $\rho_f$ and viscosity $\eta$:

1. Viscous Drag (Stokes' Law):
$$F_v = 6\pi \eta r v$$
2. Equilibrium at Terminal Speed:

Net Force = $0 \implies F_v + F_B = W$

$$6\pi\eta r v_T + \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \rho_f g = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \rho_s g$$
3. Terminal Velocity Formula:
$$v_T = \frac{2 r^2 g (\rho_s - \rho_f)}{9 \eta}$$

Key Terminal Velocity Takeaways:

• $v_T \propto r^2$ (Velocity increases quadratically with radius).

• If $\rho_s < \rho_f$ (e.g. Air bubble rising in water), $v_T$ is negative $\implies$ Bubble moves UPWARDS.

Reynolds Number ($Re$) & Flow Regimes

$$Re = \frac{\text{Inertial Force}}{\text{Viscous Force}} = \frac{\rho v D}{\eta}$$
Re < 2000

Streamline / Laminar Flow

2000 < Re < 3000

Unsteady / Transition Flow

Re > 3000

Turbulent Flow

4. High-Yield JEE Traps & Common Mistakes

Avoid these classic conceptual and calculation errors in JEE Main & Advanced

Trap #1: Free Surfaces Count

Forgetting $N=2$ for Soap Bubbles

Always check if it is a Liquid Drop ($\Delta P = 2S/R$) or a Soap Bubble ($\Delta P = 4S/R$). For work done blowing a soap bubble, remember to multiply total surface area by $2$!

Trap #2: Sudden Loading Extension

Factor of 2 in Sudden Loading

Under sudden loading, max extension is $\mathbf{2 \times \frac{FL}{AY}}$, NOT $\frac{FL}{AY}$. Max stress is also doubled! in an ideal undamped system, energy is not automatically lost as heat.

Trap #3: Capillary Insufficient Length

Water Does NOT Overflow!

In a capillary tube shorter than calculated rise $h$, liquid will NEVER spill over like a fountain. It rises to the top edge and increases its radius of curvature $R'$ according to Jurin's law ($h R = h' R'$).

Trap #4: Viscosity Units

Confusing Poise and Poiseuille

$1\text{ Poise} = 0.1\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$. If viscosity is given in Poise in JEE questions, multiply by $0.1$ before plugging into SI unit equations!

Quick-reference formulas for exam-day revision

Elasticity

• $Y = \frac{F L}{A \Delta L}$

• Energy density $u = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{Stress} \times \text{Strain}$
